\section*{Abstract}\label{abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

For permutations avoiding the consecutive patterns \(123\), \(231\) and
\(312\), we identify the total number of consecutive \(321\)
occurrences, counted over all permutations and all positions, as
\(E_{n+1}-2E_n\) for every \(n\geq3\), where \(E_n\) are the Euler
zigzag numbers. Reverse-complement symmetry gives exact totals for
\(132\) and \(213\). With the normalization of Hassler and Kirgizov, the
limiting popularities are \(4/\pi^2\) for \(321\) and \(1/2-2/\pi^2\)
for each of \(132\) and \(213\). The argument marks an edge in the known
circular-difference integral-operator model. The avoidance count,
Euler-number sequence, and underlying operator are prior work. This is a
limited preprint for one consecutive-avoidance class; novelty remains
\textbf{INCONCLUSIVE}, and human peer review has \textbf{not been
performed}.

\section{Definitions and claimed
theorem}\label{definitions-and-claimed-theorem}

Let \(A_n=\operatorname{Av}_n(123,231,312)\) be the set of length-\(n\)
permutations avoiding the three indicated \textbf{consecutive} patterns.
Let \(E_n\) be the Euler up/down (zigzag) numbers characterized by

\(A(z)=\sum_{n\geq0} E_n z^n/n!=\sec z+\tan z\).

Let \(N_n(p)\) be the sum of the numbers of consecutive occurrences of
pattern \(p\) over \textbf{all} permutations in \(A_n\), counting each
location separately.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}

\textbf{Theorem (independent AI mathematical audit reported PASS;
novelty not established).} For every integer \(n\geq3\),

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \(|A_n|=nE_{n-1}\) (known, included only as a cross-check).
\item
  \(N_n(321)=E_{n+1}-2E_n\).
\item
  \(N_n(132)=N_n(213)=\frac{(n-2)nE_{n-1}-E_{n+1}+2E_n}{2}\).
\item
  Using the original paper's normalization
  \(\operatorname{pop}(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}N_n(p)/(n|A_n|)\), whenever
  the limit is evaluated: \(\operatorname{pop}(321)=4/\pi^2\),
  \(\operatorname{pop}(132)=\operatorname{pop}(213)=1/2-2/\pi^2\).
\end{enumerate}

Since \(\pi\) is transcendental, the three limits above are irrational
(indeed transcendental, since a nonzero rational affine transform of a
transcendental number is transcendental).

\section{Uniform real variables and circular
differences}\label{uniform-real-variables-and-circular-differences}

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) be independent \(\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)\)
random variables. Almost surely they are distinct, and their rank
permutation is uniformly distributed on \(S_n\). Define

\(u_j=\{x_j-x_{j+1}\}\), for \(1\leq j\leq n-1\),

where braces denote fractional part in \([0,1)\).

The map \((x_1,\ldots,x_n)\longmapsto(x_1,u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1})\) is an
invertible triangular group transformation on the torus modulo one,
preserving normalized Lebesgue measure. Therefore the anchor \(x_1\) and
all \(u_j\) are mutually independent \(\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)\) random
variables. Conditional on the entire \(u\) vector, each \(x_j\) is
uniform on \([0,1)\).

\textbf{Local equivalence:} for any triple of distinct reals
\(x_j,x_{j+1},x_{j+2}\), the associated rank pattern is in
\(\{132,213,321\}\) if and only if \(u_j+u_{j+1}<1\). The other three
patterns \(\{123,231,312\}\) correspond to \(u_j+u_{j+1}>1\). Equality
occurs only on a measure-zero set.

Indeed, the three positions on the circle are \(x_j\), \(\{x_j-u_j\}\),
and \(\{x_j-u_j-u_{j+1}\}\). When \(u_j+u_{j+1}<1\), the rank pattern is
\(321\) precisely when \(x_j\geq u_j+u_{j+1}\), a conditional event of
probability \(1-u_j-u_{j+1}\).

Write \(m=n-1\),
\(\Omega_m=\{u\in[0,1]^m: u_j+u_{j+1}<1\ \text{for }j=1,\ldots,m-1\}\).
It follows that

\(|A_n|/n!=\int_{\Omega_{n-1}}1\,du\), and

\(N_n(321)/n!=\int_{\Omega_{n-1}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(1-u_j-u_{j+1})\right]du\).

\section{Resolvent and Euler EGF}\label{resolvent-and-euler-egf}

Define the integral operator on continuous functions

\[(Uf)(t)=\int_0^{1-t}f(s)\,ds\]

and its resolvent applied to \(1\) as a power series

\(F(z,t)=\sum_{k\geq0}z^k(U^k1)(t)\).

Initially the series converges for \(|z|<1\), which suffices to justify
term-by-term integration and differentiation in \(t\) locally near
\(z=0\). The identity \(F=1+zUF\) gives

\(\frac{\partial F(z,t)}{\partial t}=-zF(z,1-t)\), \(F(z,1)=1\), and
\(\frac{\partial^2F}{\partial t^2}=-z^2F\).

Solving yields

\(F(z,t)=A(z)\cos(zt)-\sin(zt)\),

where \(A(z)=(1+\sin z)/\cos z=\sec z+\tan z\), since \(F(z,1)=1\).

For \(m\geq1\), the integral
\(\int_{\Omega_m}1\,du=\int_0^1(U^{m-1}1)(t)\,dt\). Consequently

\(\sum_{m\geq1}z^m\operatorname{vol}(\Omega_m)=z\int_0^1F(z,t)\,dt=F(z,0)-1=A(z)-1\).

Coefficient comparison yields \(\operatorname{vol}(\Omega_m)=E_m/m!\),
and hence the known \(|A_n|=nE_{n-1}\).

\textbf{Audit checkpoint B:} Check both boundary conditions, the series
index \(m=n-1\), and the normalizing \(n!\).

\section{Marking a 321 occurrence and the exact
EGF}\label{marking-a-321-occurrence-and-the-exact-egf}

Set

\(J(z)=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-u}(1-u-v)F(z,u)F(z,v)\,dv\,du\).

Choosing a marked adjacent pair \(u_j,u_{j+1}\) in an admissible chain
\(u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1}\), the chain on either side of that edge
contributes powers of \(U\). Thus the product of the two \(F\) factors
enumerates all left/right extensions. Since the two marked variables
contribute \(z^2\), while the original occurrence-count EGF is indexed
by permutation length \(n=m+1\), we get the \textbf{crucial shift}

\(H(z):=\sum_{n\geq3}N_n(321)z^n/n!=z^3J(z)\).

The following algebra evaluates the integral exactly. From
\(F(z,t)=1+z\int_0^{1-t}F(z,s)\,ds\), first obtain

\(z\int_0^{1-u}(1-u-v)F(z,v)\,dv=\int_u^1(F(z,y)-1)\,dy\).

Therefore, interchanging integration order,

\[J(z)=\frac1z\int_0^1F(z,u)\int_u^1(F(z,y)-1)\,dy\,du\]

\[\phantom{J(z)}=\frac1z\int_0^1(F(z,y)-1)\int_0^y F(z,u)\,du\,dy\]

\(\phantom{J(z)}=\frac1{z^2}\int_0^1(F(z,y)-1)(F(z,1-y)-1)\,dy\).

Use \(F_y(z,y)=-z F(z,1-y)\), \(F(z,0)=A(z)\), \(F(z,1)=1\), and
\(z\int_0^1F(z,y)\,dy=A(z)-1\). Then

\[z^3J(z)=-\int_0^1(F-1)F_y\,dy-z\int_0^1(F-1)\,dy\]

\[\phantom{z^3J(z)}=(A-1)^2/2-(A-1)+z\]

\(\phantom{z^3J(z)}=(A^2-4A+3)/2+z\).

Finally \(A'=(A^2+1)/2\), as follows by differentiating
\(\sec z+\tan z\). Therefore

\(H(z)=A'(z)-2A(z)+1+z\).

\needspace{4\baselineskip}

The coefficients of \(z^0,z^1,z^2\) are zero. Comparing the coefficient
of \(z^n/n!\) for every \(n\geq3\) proves

\(N_n(321)=E_{n+1}-2E_n\).

\textbf{Audit checkpoint C:} The most important possible failure points
are the \(z^3\) shift in \(H\), swapping integrals, and the last
integration by parts. Check each separately, without relying on
numerical data.

\section{The two other allowed
patterns}\label{the-two-other-allowed-patterns}

Reverse-complement (\(R\circ C\)) fixes the forbidden set
\(\{123,231,312\}\) (it fixes \(123\) and swaps \(231\) and \(312\)),
and exchanges the permitted patterns \(132\) and \(213\), while fixing
\(321\). Hence \(N_n(132)=N_n(213)\) exactly for all \(n\).

Every admissible permutation contributes \(n-2\) consecutive triples,
all in \(\{132,213,321\}\); therefore

\(N_n(132)+N_n(213)+N_n(321)=(n-2)|A_n|\).

Combining this relation with the established expression for \(N_n(321)\)
proves claim 3.

\section{Asymptotic normalization (important
correction)}\label{asymptotic-normalization-important-correction}

Hassler--Kirgizov (arXiv:2511.02442v2, their Eq. (1.1)) use \(n|A_n|\)
as the denominator in their definition of \(\operatorname{pop}\). The
alternative \((n-2)|A_n|\) is a natural normalization over actual
length-three windows but differs at finite \(n\); both have the same
limit.

The standard asymptotic of Euler zigzag numbers is

\(E_n/n!\sim2(2/\pi)^{n+1}\).

Hence, using \(|A_n|=nE_{n-1}\),

\(\frac{N_n(321)}{n|A_n|}=\frac{E_{n+1}-2E_n}{n^2E_{n-1}}\longrightarrow\frac4{\pi^2}\).

The other two limits follow from symmetry and the sum of \((n-2)|A_n|\)
windows. This gives both a solution candidate for their open Class 12
and an irrational asymptotic popularity example.

\section{Prior work and scope of the
contribution}\label{prior-work-and-scope-of-the-contribution}

Hassler and Kirgizov {[}1{]}, Table 1, list this exact
consecutive-avoidance class as Class 12; their equation (1.1) supplies
the denominator \(n|A_n|\). Their v2 asks for this class's popularities.
The statement addresses this class and length-three consecutive
statistics only.

The count \(|A_n|=nE_{n-1}\) is due to Kitaev and Mansour {[}2{]} and is
explicitly restated and attributed in Ehrenborg, Kitaev and Perry
{[}3{]}, Theorem 7.5. Section 7 of {[}3{]} supplies the
circular-difference invariant subspace and integral-operator framework;
these ingredients are not claimed as new. Zhao {[}4{]} studies classical
occurrences at arbitrary increasing index positions, so its
identical-looking forbidden triples describe a different problem. OEIS
A000708 {[}5{]} already records \(E_{n+1}-2E_n\) and the EGF
\((1-2\cos z)/(1-\sin z)\); neither the sequence nor that EGF is claimed
as new.

The potential contribution is their exact identification with the marked
consecutive-\(321\) total in this class, together with the three
resulting popularity constants. A targeted failure to locate a directly
duplicating theorem does not establish originality or priority.
